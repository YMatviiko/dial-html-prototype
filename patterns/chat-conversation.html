what is st Patrick's day?

St. Patrick's Day is celebrated on March 17 each year, marking the death date of Saint Patrick, the patron saint of Ireland who lived in the 5th century.

The history

Patrick wasn't actually Irish — he was born in Britain (likely Roman Britain) and was kidnapped as a teenager by Irish raiders, who enslaved him in Ireland for about six years. After escaping and returning home, he later went back to Ireland as a Christian missionary, where he's credited with spreading Christianity across the island. The holiday began as a religious feast day in the Catholic Church, commemorating his death around 461 AD.

How it's celebrated today

What started as a solemn religious observance has become a much bigger cultural celebration, especially outside Ireland:

  • Wearing green (tied to Ireland's nickname "the Emerald Isle" and shamrocks)
  • Parades — the biggest ones are actually in the U.S., with New York City's parade being one of the oldest and largest in the world
  • Shamrocks, which legend says Patrick used to explain the Holy Trinity to pagan converts
  • Beer dyed green, Irish food like corned beef and cabbage, and general festivities

Fun facts

  • Chicago famously dyes its river green every year for the occasion.
  • The "no snakes in Ireland" legend attributed to Patrick is symbolic (there never were snakes there natively) rather than literal.
  • It's a public holiday in Ireland, Northern Ireland, and Montserrat.
